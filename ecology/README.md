# An evolving ecology

A grassland with animals on it, and nothing about those animals is fixed. Each carries four numbers - legs, body, mouth, eyes - that are inherited with mutation, and every one of them is a cost as well as a capability. What eats what, how big anything gets, and whether the community ends up crowded or bare are outcomes of that arithmetic, not settings.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

The laboratory version, with every coefficient exposed before you run it, is at <a href="https://worldbyjoe.github.io/selection-abm/">selection-abm</a>.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- The genome IS the animal, and it is drawn as ink: legs, body and mouth are cyan, magenta and yellow, eyes are black, so an animal's colour tells you its build at a glance and two animals that look alike are alike.
- Every trait is paid for twice - once to build and once to keep. A newborn costs its parents 3.0 per unit of legs, 4.5 of body and 8.7 of mouth, plus the fat it is sent out with; then it burns 0.75, 1.8 and 0.3 of those every step it stays alive. A big mouth is the most expensive thing an animal can own, which is why carnivores are rare and never the default.
- Predation is a diet, not a species. An animal eats another if its mouth can take that body - and across 43 long runs every one of the 2,787 carnivores alive at the end descended from a founder that grazed. Founding hunters died out. Carnivory in this world was always something that evolved, never something that arrived.
- Being hidden is a size problem. An animal is concealed in grass taller than it is, so the grass cap decides which body plans can ever hide - raise it and the refuge extends to everyone, which looks like a result about body size and is really a result about the ceiling.
- Provisioning is what makes a species. A parent gives a newborn either five steps of its own upkeep or ten, and the two do not interbreed, so the labels on screen are genuine reproductive boundaries rather than bins.
- The world pushes back. Patches of grass regrow at their own rates, four impassable ridges cut the map with gaps to find, and about every six hundred steps a disturbance lands somewhere, killing 45% of the animals caught in it and stripping the grass. Long runs usually end in monodominance - one lineage holding the field - and the interesting question is which one, and why.
