Wall pieces

Screens written for a television on a wall - each one a simulation running live in the browser rather than a recording. Open any of them; there is nothing to install.

Finding a gas leak from a moving vehicle

A car drives a real street network with a methane analyser on the roof. Gas disperses from hidden leaks as Gaussian puffs carried on a wandering wind; the analyser samples whatever concentration is at the car. Colour on the ground is not the gas - it is the survey's BELIEF about where the leak is, built up reading by reading.

Why surnames die out

Five hundred families, one surname each, passed from father to son and to nobody else. Some men have no sons. Their name is gone, and nothing in a patrilineal system can bring it back. Watch long enough and five hundred names become one.

An n-body system, integrated honestly

Bodies pulling on each other under Newtonian gravity, integrated forward with no scripted orbits and no choreography. What you see is what the equations do.

Slime mould finds the network

Millions of particles, each doing nothing cleverer than steering towards the strongest smell in front of it and leaving a trail of its own. Out of that comes a transport network - the same behaviour that lets Physarum polycephalum reproduce a country's rail map when food is placed at the cities.

Two chemicals, and every animal pattern

A Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion system: one chemical feeds, another consumes it and reproduces, both spread at different rates. Change two numbers and the same equations give spots, stripes, mazes, waves, or dividing cells - which is Turing's 1952 answer to how a uniform embryo becomes a patterned animal.

A tree grown from its carbon budget

Not a fractal and not an L-system. Every shoot is paid for out of what the leaves earned that season, against the cost of building and maintaining the wood already standing. The shape is what is left over.

A day of sun and wind over real ground

Eighteen hours behind and eighteen ahead, over one of three regions. The land is veiled in grey and sunlight lifts the veil, so the map shows through exactly where the sun is reaching the ground. The streaks are surface wind, drawn by letting tracers ride the field.

The stars on the way

Every run picks a real planetary system and flies to it. It opens on our corner of the Milky Way with both ends of the trip marked in it, then takes the 4,737 stars known to carry planets and peels them away, farthest first, until only the handful actually on the way is left - the rungs of the ladder out from here in that particular direction.

The fastest line down a mountain

A fresh trail is invented for every run - a bench cut traversing a hillside, switchbacking and plunging through changing dirt - and a rider is simulated down it. Nothing is scripted: a planner chooses where to be across the trail and when to brake, out of physics, and then the bike has to actually follow that plan.
Every piece is a single HTML file with no build step, no server and no API keys, generated from the version that actually runs on the wall.
