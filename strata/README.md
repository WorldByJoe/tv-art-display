# Strata

A stack of sedimentary beds - sandstone, limestone, siltstone, mudstone, shale - is folded, planed off, and then rained on for a million years or so. Hard beds hold up cliffs and fins while the soft ones strip away, and the camera circles slowly as the canyons cut back from the front edge. Every landscape is new: seven minutes, then another folds.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- The model is a stream-power landscape evolution model on a 320 by 200 grid of 50 m cells: rain (a base rate, more on high ground, plus drifting wet belts) is routed downhill by a priority flood, rivers cut in proportion to the square root of their discharge and their slope, with the erodibility of whichever bed is exposed, and every cell slumps to the critical angle of its rock - shale to about 17 degrees, sandstone to 50. That last rule is what makes the cliff-and-bench profile.
- About half of the worlds carry a tight fold whose limbs dip 65 to 80 degrees; the plane cut across its crest exposes every bed as a narrow stripe, and the hard stripes erode into spines.
- The picture is a heightfield ray-marched column by column, with every pixel of a cliff coloured by the bed at that elevation, so the faces show their stratigraphy. Press i for the seed, the beds and the model settings; n starts a new world.
