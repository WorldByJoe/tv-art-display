# The fastest line down a mountain

A fresh trail is invented for every run - a bench cut traversing a hillside, switchbacking and plunging through changing dirt - and a rider is simulated down it. Nothing is scripted: a planner chooses where to be across the trail and when to brake, out of physics, and then the bike has to actually follow that plan.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Pin a run with <code>?seed=4242&amp;rider=pro&amp;mode=known</code> - rider is <code>pro</code> or <code>weekend</code>, mode is <code>known</code> or <code>onsight</code>. Keys: <code>n</code> new trail, <code>m</code> the same trail in the other state of knowledge, <code>r</code> the other rider, <code>c</code> camera, <code>p</code> pause, <code>t</code> physics self-test.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer.

## What it is doing

- The line painted on the ground ahead is the plan, coloured by intention: red for braking, green for pedalling, white for coasting. Watch it change as the rider commits to it - and watch them slide when the plan asks for more grip than the dirt has.
- Two riders and two states of knowledge, rolled per run. A PRO against a WEEKEND rider, and KNOWS THE TRAIL - a practised race run, planned end to end - against ON-SIGHT, where the rider can only plan as far as they can SEE and must always be able to slow down before the trail disappears round a corner. On-sight is markedly slower, which is the whole point.
- Seven surfaces from hardpack to deep loose to rock slab, each with its own friction and its own roughness; cambered and bermed corners; rocks in the riding line that the rider decides to roll or go around, with the rejected choice drawn as a ghost line carrying what it would have cost.
- The physics it is made of: a traction circle on every surface, banked-turn cornering, the pitch-over limit that stops you braking harder than the rear wheel will stay down, weight transfer, drag by body posture, and suspension-filtered roughness. Press <code>t</code> for the self-tests it has to keep passing.
