# The fastest line down a mountain

A fresh trail is invented for every run - a bench cut traversing a hillside, switchbacking and plunging through changing dirt, with jumps built into it - and a rider is simulated down it. Nothing is scripted: a planner chooses where to be across the trail and when to brake, out of physics, and then the bike has to actually follow that plan.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Pin the run with <code>?rider=</code> (pro, weekend), <code>?mode=</code> (known, onsight) and <code>?seed=</code>.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- The line painted on the ground ahead is the plan, coloured by intention: red for braking, green for pedalling, white for coasting. Watch it change as the rider commits to it - and watch them slide when the plan asks for more grip than the dirt has.
- Two riders and two states of knowledge, rolled per run. A PRO against a WEEKEND rider, and KNOWS THE TRAIL - a practised race run, planned end to end - against ON-SIGHT, which can only plan as far as it can see. The run opens with one sentence naming the trail, its length and drop, the rider and which of the two they are; after that the only words are the readout.
- The panel is live telemetry, not decoration: speed in miles per hour, the grade under the wheels, how much of the available grip is being spent, and what the planner is currently asking for. When the rider slides, the grip figure is what went wrong.
- Jumps are a first-class part of the trail rather than an accident of the terrain, and a run that reaches most of its vertical without any is given some.
