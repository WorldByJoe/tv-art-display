# The stars on the way

Every run picks a real planetary system and flies to it. It opens on our corner of the Milky Way with both ends of the trip marked in it, then takes the 4,737 stars known to carry planets and peels them away, farthest first, until only the handful actually on the way is left - the rungs of the ladder out from here in that particular direction.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Pin a destination with <code>?host=</code>, using any host name in the archive with three or more known planets: TRAPPIST-1, KOI-351, Kepler-186, K2-219, Barnard's star.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer.

## What it is doing

- The corridor is a cylinder around the line from the Sun to the destination, opened until it holds nine planet-bearing stars. Measured across all 346 reachable systems that radius comes out between 6 and 33 light years, median 11, and it barely moves whether the crossing is 40 light years or 6,600. Watch a few runs and the spacing of the ladder in different directions is the thing you come away with.
- The data is real and baked in: a snapshot of the NASA Exoplanet Archive, with sky positions and distances for every host star and full parameters for every system with three or more known planets. The cone-shaped crowd in the deep field IS the discovery record - four years of Kepler staring at one patch of sky - and the farthest stars of all were found by looking straight into the galactic bulge.
- Two of the three lies are cartographic and both are named on screen. Radius is log-squeezed, so one picture can hold a neighbour four light years away and the centre of the Galaxy at 26,700. Across the corridor the scale is stretched six times while you are looking at it from outside, or eleven light years of width against six hundred of length would be a hairline; the panel says which is in force.
- The third lie is the engine. The departure is real physics - a dive past Jupiter, which is how every probe that has left the Solar System left. The crossing is not: no drive we have covers hundreds of light years. The ship flies the constant-1g brachistochrone of hard science fiction, and the two clocks in the panel are worked out with actual special relativity - ship years from rapidity, Earth years from coordinate time. The fiction is the engine, never the arithmetic.
- The planets at the far end are drawn from their own measurements - radius, temperature, the light they receive - because almost no individual exoplanet has a picture, and a render driven by the numbers is more honest than a painting.
