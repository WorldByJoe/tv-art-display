# A slime mould solving a maze it cannot see

About 137,000 particles, each doing nothing cleverer than steering towards the strongest smell in front of it and leaving a trail of its own. Out of that comes a transport network - the same behaviour that lets Physarum polycephalum reproduce a country's rail map when food is placed at the cities.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

A visit shows two species and then hands on. <code>?pin</code> keeps it cycling indefinitely.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- Three rules and no fourth: sense the trail at three points ahead, turn towards the strongest, deposit. There is no planner, no global view and nothing that knows what a network is. The structure is the only stable arrangement of those rules, found a few million times a second.
- Four species, not one, and they look nothing alike: Veins, Lace, Roots and Turbulence differ in how wide and how far ahead they sense, how sharply they turn, and how fast the trail decays. Every 48 seconds the field is re-seeded with a new species and a new colour ramp, both drawn at random - so the same one can come up twice.
- The particles themselves are drawn as a pale swarm over the trail field. One in sixteen is shown, about 8,500 dots, because drawing the trail alone left the 'each particle' of the description invisible.
