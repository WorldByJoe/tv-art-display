# Why surnames die out

Five hundred families, one surname each, passed from father to son and to nobody else. Some men have no sons. Their name is gone, and nothing in a patrilineal system can bring it back. Watch long enough and five hundred names become one.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- Each act opens on the village - five hundred men as squares, coloured by the name they carry - which holds the frame for nine seconds, dissolves over six, and does not come back. What it dissolves into is the river: a Muller plot running the full frame, time left to right, each band one surname, its thickness the share of living men who carry it. Read backwards it is the coalescent tree of the male line.
- Three acts. DRIFT is the neutral model: nothing but the luck of sons. WEALTH gives fortune the same inheritance path as the name, and drift turns into selection - during that opening a man's brightness is his rank in the wealth distribution, so you can see who is about to out-reproduce whom. INVENTION adds a trickle of newly coined names, each one flaring white in the village and in its first column of the river.
- One number is on screen while a run goes: how many surnames are still alive. It falls fast and then slowly, which is the whole point.
- When eight names are left the run pauses, zooms, and gives the survivors real European surnames - and then goes on running to fixation with the labels attached and tracking their bands. The endgame is the part worth watching, and it is after the pause, not before it.
- The history is compressed continuously rather than in jumps, so the twenty-generation cliff and the several-hundred-generation crawl are both on screen at once. The run also speeds up as it goes - up to thirty times its opening rate - because the first twenty generations contain most of the story.
- Each act has a hard time limit and an unlucky drift run can be curtained off with a dozen names still standing. That is honest: fixation is certain eventually, not soon.
