# Why surnames die out

Five hundred families, one surname each, passed from father to son and to nobody else. Some men have no sons. Their name is gone, and nothing in a patrilineal system can bring it back. Watch long enough and five hundred names become one.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer.

## What it is doing

- The top half is a Muller plot: time runs left to right and each band is one surname, its thickness the share of living men who carry it. Read backwards it is the coalescent tree of the male line. The bottom strip is the same generation drawn as people rather than as area.
- Three acts. DRIFT is the neutral model. WEALTH gives fortune the same inheritance path as the name, and drift turns into selection. INVENTION adds a trickle of newly coined names, which is why real surname pools have never collapsed to one - it settles at about nineteen names, close to the Ewens sampling formula's prediction of theta*ln(1 + N/theta).
- When eight names are left the run stops, the view drops the crowded opening, and the survivors are given real European surnames - because by then the piece is about particular families rather than about a statistic.
- The time axis halves itself whenever it fills, so the twenty-generation cliff and the several-hundred-generation crawl are both visible at once. Every generation always occupies the same width as every other.
