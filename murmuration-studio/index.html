Murmuration studio

Render your own video of a simulated starling murmuration: up to 100,000 birds, in 4K, entirely on this computer. Every bird follows the same rules, drawn from field studies of starlings; there is no leader.

Best in Chrome or Edge on a desktop or laptop. They can stream a large 4K video straight to a folder. Safari and Firefox can make videos too, but must hold the whole file in memory, so keep those to 1080p, under a minute and under 20,000 birds. Keep this tab open and in front while it renders: browsers slow down background tabs.

About the model

Each bird steers only by what it perceives. Every cue is a turning torque, so turns carry inertia. Each step, a bird:

  • matches the heading and speed of its 6 or 7 nearest neighbours, ignoring any in a blind sector behind it (Ballerini et al. 2008; Bialek et al. 2014);
  • keeps its personal space and closes on neighbours that drift off;
  • turns gently toward the edges of the flock as it sees it against the sky (Pearce et al. 2014);
  • is drawn back toward the roost and a preferred height, as in StarDisplay (Hildenbrandt et al. 2010).

At dusk each bird goes down at its own moment, sooner once two neighbours have. A settings file saved beside each video records every value used.
