# The Race

A mountain-bike race on a landscape that did not exist a minute ago. First the land: folded sedimentary beds rained on for three hundred thousand years, then frozen. Then the course: one lap of 4-7 miles laid round the map through alternately high and low ground, the singletrack benched into the hillsides with switchbacks where the grade cap demands them, two-track on the gentle ground, and only cliffs off limits. Then eight riders with their traits, and the race at four times real time: an overview of the whole course with a chart of every rider's place against distance, a 3D chase window on the moment that matters, and a board of what each rider is doing on which rock.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- Every rider has an engine from exercise physiology: aerobic power they can hold for an hour, a reserve of 10-35 kJ to spend above it that refills only when they ease off, and a sprint. Fibre type trades these off as muscle biopsies show (fast-twitch: a bigger reserve and sprint, slow recovery, more heat; slow-twitch: aerobic power, quick recovery), body mass trades watts for watts per kilo, and race craft decides when the reserve is spent: on a pass, before the singletrack, in the run-in. The course gives it room: a long two-track start, passing places so no singletrack runs much past 2 km, and a two-track run-in to the line.
- Every rider is a physics model: watts against gravity, rolling drag by rock type and air; a true cornering limit from grip and curvature, perceived with an error that shrinks with skill and grows with fatigue; a slide when a bend is entered too fast - down for a few seconds on open ground, out over an edge. On a descent the speed is set by skill: the speed a rider can still stop from on that rock at that steepness, so a novice is slow on loose steep ground and walks the worst of it. Riders are drawn from one category (3.4-4.4 W/kg), so climbing power is not the whole story. Singletrack holds a faster rider behind a slower one; passing happens only on the two-track. Each rider is also a heat balance: metabolic heat, the sun, convection, sweat capped by what humid air will take, a core temperature that throttles power past a threshold heat tolerance raises; acclimatised riders also sweat sooner and lose no power to cardiovascular strain on a hot day.
- The chase window is WebGL: the frozen surface as a lit mesh coloured by the rock bed at every height, terrain shadows from the day's sun, the trail cut into the hillside as real geometry (tread level across, cut bank above, fill below), juniper and sage, and each rider pedalling a bike in their colour.
- The results cards rank the field with calories burned, litres sweated, feet climbed and peak core temperature. Press n for a new land, i for the seed and the model settings.
