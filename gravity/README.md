# An n-body system, integrated honestly

Bodies pulling on each other under Newtonian gravity, with no scripted orbits. Five scenarios - a protoplanetary disk, a binary dance, two clusters colliding, an Oort scattering, a tidal encounter - one drawn at random each run, each with its own initial conditions. Bodies that touch MERGE, conserving mass and momentum, so the count only ever falls.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

Press `i` for the parameters it is running, and the arrows appear if you move
a pointer. A standalone copy starts itself again when a run ends; add
`?pin` to the URL to keep the current run going instead.

## What it is doing

- Colour is speed divided by the local escape velocity, on a turbo ramp, so red means leaving forever at any radius - and the reddest body on screen is labelled ESCAPING once it passes 92% of escape. The bar across the top is the system's mass split among its bodies, largest first: it starts as a comb of hundreds of slivers and resolves into a few broad blocks.
- A merger flashes. The brightness and size of the flare are the logarithm of the kinetic energy the collision destroyed, measured against the median for that scenario, so a glancing touch and a head-on annihilation do not look alike.
- Watch for a body whose spin axis lies across your line of sight: its terminator sweeps rather than rotates. The projection is real - the camera orbits the system - and it is the only thing on screen that is not a consequence of the equations.
- The physics is honest; the picture is not, in three stated places. A body's collision radius and its drawn radius are separate constants, so the disc you see is not the target. Densities are chosen for what the accretion rate needs, not from any density model. And during the closing pull-back the minimum drawn radius grows about two and a half times, so distant companions do not vanish.
- The run is on a clock, not on a physical condition: three minutes, then a twenty-six second eased pull-back to reveal whatever is still bound and has been off-frame the whole time, and one closing sentence with the ledger - how many bodies started, how many are still bound to each other, and how many were thrown clear by slingshots carrying what share of the mass.
