# Kite Flying

A person in a big grassy field flies a kite in a wind that never holds still: a diamond with a tail, a two-line delta they steer, or a Chinese centipede dragon of twenty discs. They walk it up off the ground, let line out, run when the wind drops, and in some scenes a tree is waiting to eat it.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That is the whole of it - one file, no build,
no server, no keys. It is written for a 4K screen and scales down.

One small line at the bottom left gives the wind, the pull on the line in pounds, how much line is out and how high the kite is. The up arrow (or the on-screen `i`) opens a card with more numbers; while it is open, left and right choose the kite (any, diamond, delta or dragon). In the URL: `?kite=diamond|delta|dragon`, `?tree=on|off`, `?wind=` for the mean wind in m/s, `?seed=` with any number to repeat a scene; `n` starts a new scene.

## What it is doing

- Everything flies by physics, not by animation. The kite is a rigid frame of flat sails; each sail feels lift and drag from the wind it meets, worked out on four patches so a tumbling kite is slowed by its own spin. The line is a chain of short segments that sags, stretches a little and catches the wind along its length.
- The wind is gusty in space as well as in time: eddies from 3 to 190 m across drift downwind through the field, and it blows harder with height, as it does over grass. On top of that the weather is scripted: lulls, gusts and slow swings in direction.
- A single-line kite steers itself. What keeps it centred is the bow of its cross spar (dihedral), not the tail; in testing, a longer tail made it wander more.
- The two-line flyer has an intention (sweeping back and forth across the wind, up and down, figure eights, or resting the kite overhead) and turns it by pulling one line, which brakes that wingtip. A gust or a lull in the middle of a turn can put the kite in the grass, and then they walk out and relaunch it.
- The centipede is a kite train: a head and twenty 40 cm discs on strings, each disc flying as its own small kite at a fixed angle, rocking slightly so ripples run down the body. Big ones pull hard; this one pulls about 16 lb in a 15 mph wind.
- Launches are slow on purpose: the flyer walks back with the line fixed, tugging now and then, until the kite has real height, then lets line out in spurts. Steady flight takes about half a minute, longer in light air.
